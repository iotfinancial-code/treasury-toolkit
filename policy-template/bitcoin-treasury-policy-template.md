## 1. Purpose

This policy sets the limits, controls and responsibilities for holding bitcoin as part of [Company]'s treasury reserves. Its aim is to protect the Company's ability to meet its obligations while any bitcoin is held.

## 2. Authority

This policy is approved by the board (or the directors, where there is no formal board). Changes to sections 3, 4 and 5 require the same approval. The policy owner is responsible for day to day compliance.

## 3. Protected reserves

Before any bitcoin is acquired, and at all times while it is held, the Company keeps in cash or cash equivalents at least:

- [N] months of operating costs, plus
- all known obligations due in that period, including tax liabilities, payroll and loan repayments.

Protected reserves are recalculated at each review in section 9.

## 4. Allocation limits

- Excess reserves means operating cash minus protected reserves.
- Bitcoin holdings may not exceed [X]% of excess reserves, measured at cost at the time of each acquisition.
- [Optional] Bitcoin holdings may not exceed [amount] at cost. Where both limits apply, the lower one applies.
- If, at a review, fair value exceeds the percentage limit by more than [Y] percentage points, the policy owner reports this to the approvers, who decide whether to rebalance and record the decision.
- If operating cash falls below protected reserves, no further acquisitions are made and the approvers meet to decide next steps.
- No borrowing, leverage, lending of bitcoin, staking, or derivatives are permitted under this policy.

## 5. Approvals

- Named approvers: [Role 1], [Role 2], [Role 3].
- Every acquisition, disposal or transfer requires approval by [M] of [N] named approvers.
- The person who initiates a transaction may not be the only approver.
- Approvals are recorded in writing (email or minutes) before execution.
- Changes to approvers are approved under section 2 and recorded in the key register.

## 6. Custody

Custody model: [Regulated custodian / Collaborative multisig (2 of 3) / Self-managed multisig (2 of 3) / Exchange-traded product (spot bitcoin ETF or crypto ETN) held in a brokerage account].

Note: a single-signature wallet (one key on one device) is not listed. IoT Financial does not recommend it for a company treasury, because one device or one person becomes a single point of failure for loss, theft or a device flaw.

[Describe the arrangement: who holds which key or account, by role.]

- A key register records each key or account, its holder (by role), its location and the date it was last tested.
- A recovery test using a small amount is carried out at least annually and after any change of signer.
- Recovery phrases are never photographed, typed into a computer, or stored in cloud services.
- Treasury holdings are not kept in a single-signature wallet, where one device or one person would be a single point of failure.
- Device firmware is kept up to date from the maker's official source, and the policy owner checks the maker's security advisories.
- Where a third party is used, the policy owner reviews its terms, controls and status at least annually.

If an exchange-traded product is used instead of holding bitcoin directly:

- The Company holds shares or notes, which are securities, not bitcoin. It holds no private keys, and it cannot withdraw bitcoin from the product or move it on chain. In this policy, bitcoin holdings include these products.
- The key register is replaced by an account register: each brokerage account, who can place trades and who can withdraw cash (by role), and the date access was last reviewed.
- The policy owner reviews the broker's and the product's terms, fees and status at least annually.

## 7. Accounting and records

- US clause: if the Company reports under US GAAP, holdings in scope of FASB ASU 2023-08 are measured at fair value, with changes included in net income each reporting period. Confirm scope and presentation with the Company's own adviser.
- UK clause: the treatment depends on the framework the Company reports under (FRS 102, FRS 105, which permits historical cost only, or IFRS, where IAS 38 or IAS 2 may apply). Confirm the basis with the Company's own adviser.
- UK clause: if exposure is held through a crypto exchange-traded note or similar listed product, the Company holds a security, not bitcoin. HMRC describes cETNs as debt securities, so the accounting and Corporation Tax treatment follow the instrument rather than the cryptoasset rules. Confirm the treatment with the Company's own advisers.
- US clause: if exposure is held through a spot bitcoin exchange-traded product, the Company holds shares, which are securities, not bitcoin. They are outside the scope of FASB ASU 2023-08 and are taxed according to the product's structure as set out in its prospectus. Confirm the treatment with the Company's own advisers.
- UK clause: each transaction is recorded with its date, amount and value in pounds sterling at the time, using a consistent, documented method (HMRC Cryptoassets Manual CRYPTO40100).
- US clause: each transaction is recorded with its date, units, fair market value in US dollars at the time and basis, as listed in the IRS digital assets guidance.
- Every transaction record also includes the counterparty or address and the approval reference.
- Records are kept for at least [period agreed with your accountant].

## 8. Tax

- US clause: virtual currency is treated as property for federal tax purposes (IRS Notice 2014-21), so each disposal may give rise to a gain or loss. The Company does not rely on receiving Form 1099-DA, as corporations other than S corporations are generally exempt recipients.
- UK clause: profits and gains fall within Corporation Tax under the trading, loan relationship, intangible fixed asset or chargeable gains rules, depending on the Company's activities and accounting (HMRC CRYPTO41000). The Company gives accurate details to its cryptoasset service providers under the Cryptoasset Reporting Framework.
- The Company's tax adviser reviews the position before each year end.

## 9. Review

The policy owner reports to the approvers [monthly / quarterly / every six months / annually]:

- holdings, cost and fair value
- protected reserves and excess reserves
- the position against each limit in section 4
- any exceptions, incidents or changes to the key register

The policy itself is reviewed at least annually.

## 10. Incidents

If a key, device or account may be compromised, or a signer becomes unavailable:

1. The policy owner informs all approvers the same day.
2. All non-urgent transactions are paused.
3. The recovery procedure in the key register is followed.
4. The incident and actions taken are recorded and reported at the next review.

## 11. Communications

The Company does not make public statements that could be read as encouraging others to buy bitcoin.

- UK clause: before any public communication about holdings, the Company checks whether it could be a cryptoasset financial promotion under the FCA rules in force since 8 October 2023 (PS23/6).
- US clause: before any public communication about holdings, the Company's own legal adviser confirms whether SEC, FTC or state rules on misleading statements, or disclosure obligations, apply to what is planned.
